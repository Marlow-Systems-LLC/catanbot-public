# Lessons from our own games

Lessons learned in play. Each one was a mistake in a real game; the fix is how we play now.

## Building comes first
Points only come from pieces that actually land on the board. Before trading, check whether something can be built right now; if it can, build it.
Never trade away cards that complete a build in hand. With three ore and two grain the city gets built at once, not traded toward a third grain.
When a city is lost to the barbarians, rebuilding it matters, but not at the cost of a piece we can build this turn. Only bank toward the city when it is one or two cards away.

## Grain in the Great Canal
The Great Canal has no grain fields. Grain only comes from gold fields and the aqueduct, so grain is never offered to other players.
A knight needs wool and ore. Ask the table for wool or ore and pay with a spare card the goal does not use, never with grain.

## Trading
Offer side trades only when they move the main goal forward. A trade for brick toward a road is not worth a scarce card.
Keep a third card of a kind when it makes a 3:1 harbour trade possible, and ask for the third card when two are already in hand.
Past halfway to victory, accept only trades that are clearly good for us.

## Ships
In Seafarers games a ship costs lumber and wool and can go where roads cannot. When no land spot is reachable, sail toward the next free coast.
The first piece placed from a coastal starting spot may be a ship.

## Opening placement
Read every number token carefully before choosing a spot. A misread token turns a strong spot into a weak one; the opening decides the whole game.
In the Great Canal the last starting settlement should be on the coast, at a 3:1 harbour, and near gold.

## Reading the Great Canal board
- Every game the numbers and terrain change, except the gold fields: they always sit at the bottom of the board and always carry an 8. Gold pays any resource, so it is a grain source.
- Hexes without a number in the middle of the board are canal hexes, not farmland and not a desert.
- Read the whole board before the first placement, and read it again after every opponent placement.

## Opening seat
- Work out your seat (1st, 2nd, 3rd or 4th) from how many opponent pieces are down at your first pick.
- The 1st seat takes the single best spot, and its second pick comes last, after everyone else has picked twice.
- The last seat places the settlement and the city one after the other. Plan both together: put the city where it earns the most developments (commodities) and the best numbers.

## Opening must-haves in the Great Canal
- Aim for a 3:1 harbour. Without one the game is rarely playable.
- Starting grain is a back-up, not the aim. Take it when the last settlement gets no 3:1 harbour, or when your first settlement sits on good numbers and the right hexes and wants to become a city quickly.
- Taking two grain to build a second city first and then a knight is a last resort. It usually loses.

## Knights in Cities & Knights (Great Canal)
- A knight standing on a gold-mine corner collects any resource when that gold number rolls. Use it to get grain when nobody else has any. The science level 3 bonus (aqueduct) does the same job: a card of your choice on a roll that gives you nothing. Put a city on lumber to reach it.
- The gold knight still uses one of your knight pieces (2 basic, 2 strong, 2 mighty), so it limits how many defenders you can field.
- The knight ladder:
  1. Build one knight.
  2. Build a second as a back-up, because opponents play Deserter and Intrigue on your knights.
  3. Promote one to strong.
  4. Raise politics to level 3. Mighty knights need it.
  5. Promote to mighty, then build more knights.

## Great Canal farmland (owner 2026-10-02)
- After the start numbers are placed, the farmland tokens are turned face down. A fields hex pays no grain until the canal is fully dug, which is most of the game. Never pick an opening spot for its fields numbers; score it as if the fields were not there.
- Being on a fields hex is still a small bonus later: the Irrigation progress card (paper track) gives two grain for each fields hex next to your buildings. Remember it, don't plan the opening around it.

## Third settlement starts with a ship (owner 2026-10-02)
- In the Great Canal setup one of the three placements must be on a harbour or on the outer edge, and its first piece is a ship, not a road. When the game says "Select position for Ship", place the ship on a sea edge touching that settlement and confirm.

## Keep the city, climb for canal points (owner 2026-10-02)
- Keep the city safe: activate knights before barbarians arrive and help defend, because losing the city costs the commodity income.
- Canal points are a one-time chance in this scenario. Push for as many as you can whenever you can afford to help dig.

## Build first, then trade; use the 3:1 harbour
- On your turn, build what the hand already pays for (road, city improvement with paper/coin/cloth) before offering trades to players. Offers take time to be answered and the turn timer runs out.
- Holding a 3:1 harbour: three of a surplus card (e.g. three lumber) become one card you need (grain in Great Canal). Do it instead of holding 7+ cards into the next roll.
- 2:1 harbours exist but are rarely the deciding trade; the 3:1 is the workhorse.
- If a player re-makes an offer with different cards, judge the new offer on its own.

## First settlement on a harbour, then get the rest
A strong opening: put the first settlement on a harbour, best when that spot also has good
numbers or makes the harbour's resource. With 3:1 trading secured, use the city and the last
settlement for what is still missing (grain, numbers, gold access) rather than a second harbour.

## Gold 8 islands: knights only
In the Great Canal nothing can be built on a gold hex corner. A knight placed there collects a
card of your choice on an 8 (grain while the canal is closed). That knight is off the main
island: it does not count against the barbarians and does not dig the canal; active, it can
only chase the robber off that gold. Defend each city with knights on the main island.

## The event die: barbarians or progress cards
The third die moves the barbarian ship on 3 of its 6 faces. The other 3 faces are gates (trade, politics, science). On a gate, every player whose improvement in that colour is high enough for the red die draws a progress card: level 1 draws on red 1–2, and each level adds one more number. Raising an improvement early is not just the level ability; it is a steady flow of progress cards. A first level of paper starts the draws soonest.
You can hold at most 4 progress cards. If a 5th arrives on someone else's turn, one must be thrown away at once, so keep the least useful card as the one to go. Only the player who rolled may hold a 5th, and must play or throw away down to 4 before the turn ends. With 4 in hand, play what you can on your own turn rather than wait.

## Great Canal: where the canal runs
The canal is 9 fixed hexes, each marked with two chips. They form a path from the empty basin in the west to the lake in the east. The land under them is shuffled each game and keeps producing after it is dug. A canal hex is dug when two active knights stand on its corners, and each knight's owner scores a point. The canal is finished at the 8th hex, and then the barren fields grow grain again. Plant knights on corners shared by two canal hexes: one knight there can help dig both.

## Friendly robber setting
When the table plays with the friendly robber, the robber cannot be placed on a hex touching a player with 4 points or fewer, and those players cannot be robbed. Aim the robber at the leaders. Early in the game, when everyone has 4 points or fewer, there may be no legal target at all.

## Great Canal: grain before the first attack
The first barbarian attack comes about three turns in, and grain is scarce here. To save the city you need one wool and one ore for a knight, and one grain to make it active. As soon as you hold three of one kind and have no grain, trade them at a 3:1 harbour for grain. Do this even before the wool and ore arrive, and wait a round for those. Keep that grain for the knight; do not spend it on anything else first. The gold knight cannot help this early, and the aqueduct only rarely does.

## City walls when you keep losing cards
Walls go up before the first barbarian attack too (owner, 2026-10-04): the knight that saves the city still comes first, but do not wait for the attack to wall a city. Watch how many cards you lose to sevens and to the robber; when the losses pile up, spend 2 brick on a city wall: each wall lets you hold 2 more cards when a seven is rolled. A pillaged city loses its wall, so wall the cities your knights can hold.

## Great Canal settlements: grain or a route to gold
Grain is the scarce resource in the Great Canal. A settlement corner that touches two grain fields is worth more than a mixed corner on weak numbers. The other good choice is a 6/10 corner on the coast with a ship pointed toward the gold island, because the knight there brings in grain. Never settle on a top or outer corner that touches only one weak hex and has no route anywhere.

## One card short: bank it
When a build is one card short and no player will trade that card, use the bank. At a 3:1 harbour, trade three spare cards of one kind (for example three brick) for the missing card. A knight counts as built only when it is active, so it needs wool, ore and grain. Pay only with cards the build itself does not use. After the first trade, try a second one for the next missing card.

## Pick the trade partner who is behind
When several players accept your offer, trade with the one who has the fewest points. Never trade with a player who has more than one and a half times your points. Help the players behind you, not the leader.

## Upgrade the settlement that produces
A city doubles what its corner produces, so upgrade the settlement on the best numbers with the most different resources. Forest, pasture and mountains under a city also give commodities. In the Great Canal a corner on gold and sea produces nothing for a city, because only knights collect gold. Never put the city there while another settlement produces.

## Watch what everyone collects
Keep track of what every player gets on every roll. A player who has collected ore and wool all game most likely holds ore and wool, even when you can only see how many cards they hold. Use this to decide whom to rob: rob the player who probably holds the card you need, and never rob someone with an empty hand.

## Resource Monopoly: take what the plan needs
Resource Monopoly takes up to two cards of one resource from each player, so up to six cards. Name the resource your next build is missing, as long as the others probably hold it. If nothing is missing, name the resource that brings the most cards. After playing it, count what came in and plan the rest of the turn again. Don't sit on a Monopoly card: if it can bring two or more cards, play it.

## Spend the turn
At the end of your turn your hand should be nearly empty. Build what you can, trade at the bank or with players, and play the cards you hold. Save cards only for one named target, such as a city or the next city improvement. Then keep trading for its missing cards until no offer or bank trade is left. Bank spare cards only when the target is one or two cards short; when it is further away, trading everything away did not help in 400 simulated games, while the 1–2 short rule won 26.9% of 1200 (fair share 25%).

## The game we play is The Great Canal
The game is The Great Canal, a special scenario with Cities & Knights. It is not plain Seafarers. With 4 players you need 18 VP to win. Grain fields give nothing until the canal is finished, so grain comes only from gold, the aqueduct and the bank. Two active knights on the corners of a canal hex dig it, and each dug hex gives canal points. Ships are only for the coastal settlement and the line to the gold islands. There is no pirate, and nobody builds on the small islands.

## v0.2: walls before the first attack
Build city walls from the start, not only after the first barbarian attack. A wall on each city lets you hold 2 more cards when a seven is rolled, and a city that still has room for a wall is worth a little more. Marlow played its old plan plus early walls in 400 simulated games against its old plan and won a little more often (52.5% against a fair 50%).

## v0.2: the owner rules are kept, but as a nudge
Building knights, playing for the Defender point, routing toward gold and spending grain at once are still in Marlow's play, but at a small dose. When all of them pushed hard at the same time, Marlow won only one game in six. Combined at a small dose, with grain going to a knight only when the knight matters now, they played even with the old plan (49% in 400 games), so they stay available for the situations where they help.

## Read the bank's rates before you trade
The bank dialog shows the rate under every card (4:1, 3:1 or 2:1). Trust it over memory: a 3:1 harbour from an earlier game does not count in this one. If the rate is 4:1, do not offer three cards.

## Deserter: give up the knight you need least
When someone plays Deserter on you, you choose which knight you lose. Give up the weakest one that does nothing for you now: an idle knight, one whose canal tile is already dug, or one stuck where it cannot reach anything. Keep the knight that defends a city before the attack, collects gold, still digs an unfinished canal tile, or cuts a leader's road. Losing a knight is not always bad: you can rebuild it on a corner where it scores more. When Deserter gives you a free knight, place it like a knight you paid for: gold, an open canal tile, a city to defend, or a leader's road to cut.

## Play every useful progress card before you end the turn
Before you press End turn, play every progress card that helps you now. A card you cannot play yet must not stop you from playing the others. Hold a card only for a named target close at hand, such as a Warlord saved for the turn before an attack. Count the cards in your hand as resources when you plan: a Warlord activates all your knights for free, so never trade at the bank or with players for grain to activate knights while you hold one. Play the Warlord instead. Medicine, Engineer and Crane cut the cost of a city, wall or improvement in the same way. If a card's prompt is still waiting, such as the robber spot after a Bishop, answer it before you play the next card.

## Great Canal setup: pick the three buildings as one set
Plan the settlement, the city and the last coastal settlement together before you place the first one (owner, game 14). Count every hex by what it pays over the whole game: farmland under the canal pays almost nothing until the canal is finished, and a building never collects gold, so a lone grain 6 next to the sea is close to worthless. Put the city on the strongest wood and sheep corner, because wood gives paper and sheep gives cloth. A corner a few ship edges from a gold corner is better still, because a knight there collects gold. Use the first settlement to cover what the city lacks, such as ore and brick on a 5/9/10 corner. Once wood, sheep, brick and ore are all covered, the last settlement goes on a 3:1 harbour. In game 14 the scorer spread pips over single spots and put the city on a 5/10 harbour corner. The better set was a settlement on hills 9 with mountains 10 and 5, the city on pasture 6 with forest 9, and a 3:1 for the third.

## Setup: the prompt says which piece you are placing
In the Great Canal the order is settlement, then city with a ship, then the last coastal settlement. Read which piece the game is asking for and plan that piece. Never work it out from how many of your pieces you think are on the board. In game 15 a misread piece made the bot put the city where the settlement belonged and the settlement where the city belonged (owner).

## Block a rival's road to the canal
A road on the edge a rival needs to reach a free corner of an undug canal tile stops their knight from getting there. Do it to the player who is advancing. Expect it to be done to you: put your knight on the canal corner before a rival's road can cut the way (owner, game 15).

## Barbarians: watch the track and keep your own knight active
The barbarian ship moves along the track on the left of the board, 7 steps, then attacks and starts again. When the knights of the whole table are fewer than the cities, every player tied for the weakest defence loses a city. With 5 cities and one knight each, everyone loses one. Enough knights on the board is not safe: a rival can move (switch off) a knight just before the attack and still stay above you. So keep one active knight per city of your own. An idle knight defends nothing, so when it waits for grain, spend your cards on getting grain, not on a second knight or on promoting the idle one (owner, game 15).

- Game 15 (owner): when the game runs fast and the city is out of reach (no city on the board), share canal digs with anyone, the leader included — a shared chip is 1 VP, and if we wait the others take those hexes. With a city, keep denying the leader shared chips.

## Monopoly cards: name the resource on the picker (solo probe 2026-10-05)
Resource Monopoly and Trade Monopoly open a picker of 8 icons (lumber, brick, wool, grain, ore, cloth, coin, paper) with a green check. Pick the resource the plan needs (grain first), then press the check. Until it is confirmed, End turn stays grey. If the picker gets hidden, the glowing trade button brings it back. The picker stays open until the check is pressed, so a monopoly that keeps showing means the clicks are missing it, not that the card failed (solo 2026-10-05 22:37, fixed by sizing the clicks to the full-screen layout).

## Read the barbarian ship, not the picture (solo probe 2026-10-05)
The barbarian track has a fixed ship drawn at the start. The real barbarians are a dark ship that moves along the 7 circles. If you watch the picture you always think the attack is far away. In the probe game, Marlow built 2 knights and never activated them, and lost its only city at 06:03 while it believed the ship was at the start. When the dark ship reaches the 5th circle or later, put grain into activating knights (trade 3:1 for grain if needed) before anything else.

## Know your improvement levels: level n costs n commodities (game 17, 2026-10-05)
A city improvement costs as many commodities as the level you are buying: the 1st costs 1, the 2nd costs 2, up to 5. Count every improvement you build and price the next one from that count. In game 17 we thought every level cost 1, so we kept planning improvements we could not pay for, the game refused them 114 times, and whole turns went by with nothing built. Politics level 3 is what lets you promote knights to mighty; if you don't know your level, you can't plan the promotion. When the game greys out an improvement you can pay for at the level you think you are on, you are a level higher than you thought.

## City improvements: what each level gives and where it stops (owner, 2026-10-06)
Each level costs as many commodities as its number. Level 3 of a track gives its ability: the trading house trades commodities 2:1 with the bank, the fortress lets you promote knights to mighty, and the aqueduct gives one resource of your choice when a roll gives you nothing. Level 4 first takes that track's metropolis (+2 VP, safe from the barbarians), and level 5 takes it from a player stuck at 4. Each level also draws more progress cards: a gate roll pays you when the red die is at most your level + 1. Limits: no improvements without a city, nothing above 5, and levels 4 and 5 need a city that is not already a metropolis, unless you are raising the track whose metropolis you already hold. With a single city that is already a metropolis, the other tracks stop at 3, so build a second city before racing a second metropolis.

## Every game teaches the model, but only a better model plays (owner, 2026-10-06)
Live games and simulated games both train the model. Once per turn of a live game, Marlow writes down how every player stands. At game over that record and the winner become a training game. The new model must then beat the current one in a battle of simulated games before it takes over. If it doesn't win more than half, the current model stays and the game is kept for the next try. Every attempt is one stage in data/train/stages.jsonl, with its battle score and whether it was promoted.

## Grain before the barbarians land, by any route (solo 2026-10-06)
A knight without grain is not a defender. In this game Marlow built its knight with the barbarians at the 5th circle, then asked the table for lumber and ore while the knight stood inactive, and lost its only city to the first attack. It played the rest of the game with no city, no improvements and 6 points. When the ship is past halfway and an inactive knight would save a city, grain comes first. Ask for grain and pay 2 cards for it. If nobody gives grain, trade for the cards that complete a 3:1 set (2 cards for 2 is fine when they finish the set), then bank the set for grain. Ask for grain even if you think nobody has it: start grain, gold knights and the aqueduct all give grain.

## A refused build is out of the plan, not just out of the click (solo 2026-10-06)
Once the game refuses a build at a spot, take it out of your plan for that turn, and never trade for it. Marlow promoted its knight to strong, forgot it had, and tried to promote it again 36 times. A mighty knight needs politics level 3, and with no city it had politics 0. It even traded 3 brick for wool to pay for a promotion the game would never allow. Remember every promotion that lands: a strong knight needs the fortress (politics 3) before it can be promoted again.

## Accept a card you can't roll (owner, 2026-10-06)
When another player offers a card we roll rarely or never (only 2/3/11/12 numbers, or no field at all, like grain in the Great Canal), take it if we pay at most 2 cards each and only cards we roll well. The only exceptions are a player about to win or far ahead. Marlow refused grain three times in one game while it had none.

## Close a dead offer at once (owner, 2026-10-06)
When every player has declined, close the offer and move on. Wait up to 15 seconds only while at least one player has not answered.

## Build before you trade (owner, 2026-10-06)
When a city or settlement is affordable and the game offers a spot, build it first. Make a bank trade only with cards the affordable build doesn't use. Marlow traded until its hand was small and ended the turn with a settlement in hand.

## City before settlement, no buy-backs (owner, 2026-10-06)
When one card short of a city, trade for that card before trading for a settlement. Never pay a city's ore into a settlement. Never buy back a resource you sold earlier in the same turn. Marlow banked 3 ore for lumber, then 3 brick back into ore, and never built its second city.

## Press OK on the improvement dialog (owner, 2026-10-06)
After choosing a development, confirm the "Do you want to improve your city?" dialog with OK, then check that the commodities left the hand. If a dialog is left open it blocks every later trade and build, so cancel it.

## Smith only below the cap (owner, 2026-10-06)
Knights stop at strong (level 2) until politics is 3. Don't play the Smith on strong knights before then. Raise politics first. The bot remembers its knights across restarts.

## Take a 1-for-1 grain (owner, 2026-10-06)
In the Great Canal, take any single grain for one non-grain card when we hold no grain or a knight is inactive. The exception is a player one point from winning.

## Good harbour first, numbers last (owner, 2026-10-06)
In the Great Canal setup, when a 3:1 harbour corner has good numbers (like 8 and 9), take it with the first settlement. The last settlement then goes on the best numbers (like 5 and 6), not on a second harbour. One 3:1 is enough.

## Keep upgrading while you can pay (owner, 2026-10-06)
After a city improvement lands, check whether the commodities left pay for the next level, and buy it in the same turn. 3 paper buys science 1 and science 2.

## An idle knight comes first (owner, 2026-10-06)
When one of our knights is inactive, activating it is the first goal. Get grain before anything else. Build another knight only after every knight we have is active.

## Use the grain the moment it arrives (owner, 2026-10-06)
If an action showed red because a card was missing, look again as soon as a trade brings new cards. Marlow traded 4 ore for the grain, ended the turn with the knight still idle, and lost its city to the barbarians on the next roll.

## Keep the commodities (owner, 2026-10-06)
While a city can still use paper, coin or cloth for an improvement, never trade them away for a basic resource. They are the way to the metropolis.

## One knight per city, then keep the cards to trade
Once every city has a knight, don't build another while the first is still idle and there is no spare grain. Keep the wool and ore for the trades that bring the grain to activate it. Building a second knight with the last cards in the first round only worked out by luck. A knight on gold is the exception, since it collects without being activated.

## Take the improvement when there is a round to spare
If the commodities in hand pay for the next improvement level (3 paper for science 3), take it. Don't bank them for grain while the barbarian ship is more than 2 steps from the coast. The improvement pays for the rest of the game, and the grain for the knight can come next round. Only when the ship is about to land does the city come first.

## Trade toward the next improvement level
Improvements are worth trading for, like buildings. With 3 paper at science 3, ask a player for the 3rd wood, bank the 3 wood for the 4th paper, and take science 4 (the metropolis race). Spend a lone cloth on trade level 1 rather than holding it. Don't bank the commodities you hold when you are one card short of the next level.

## No city: rebuild it first
After the barbarians take your only city, the city comes first. Keep the 3 ore for it and never bank them for grain. Grain also goes to the city. Don't activate a knight just because grain is in hand: activate only when it digs a canal hex or takes the defender point. Don't chase sheep for new knights while there is no city to defend.

## Contest the canal: road up to a weaker knight, then push it off
In the Great Canal, a rival knight on an undug canal corner takes the dig points there. If one of your knights is stronger, or can be promoted to be stronger, build a road whose end reaches that corner. Then move your active knight along your roads and push the rival knight off. Your knight takes the corner and the dig. Do this first against the leader.

## Cities first, then settlements
When you can afford either, upgrade a settlement to a city before you place a new settlement. A city doubles the income of a spot you already hold and is the base for every improvement. When you trade, trade toward the city first and the settlement second.

## Trade first, then build
At the start of your turn, make your player offers before you build. Then plan the builds on the hand you have after the trade. A trade that arrives after the build can't change what you built. The exception: build first if the offer would give away a card the build needs.

## One bank trade at a time
A bank trade happens once. If the dialog is still open after you confirm, check your hand. If the cards moved, the trade went through, so close the dialog. Never confirm a second time, or 3 ore for 1 grain becomes 6 ore for 2 grain and the city is gone.

## Deserter on you: give up the cheapest knight
Give up the lowest-level knight that has nothing left to do, for example one sitting on a canal hex that is already dug. Between two knights of the same level, give up the inactive one. Give up an active knight instead when it is spent and the inactive one still has an undug canal tile to dig, as long as the barbarians are far away and there is time to activate again. Close to the attack, keep the knights that defend your cities.

## Trade for the improvement the dice don't bring
City improvements are worth a lot: progress cards, then the metropolis. Once you have a city, keep pushing every track to 3, then 4 and 5. Begin with the lowest track. If the next level is only one commodity short (two at levels 0 and 1) and the dice don't bring it, ask the table for it. Pay 1:1 with your least useful spare resource, but never with grain in the canal or with cards you are keeping for the city.

## Refused? Offer a different card for the same need
If the table turns down your first card for the one card your top build needs, offer another card the build doesn't use. A card you were saving for a smaller build, such as wood for a ship, counts. Example: wool for ore was refused, so offer the wood for the ore and build the knight. The knight comes before the ship, so make that trade before building the ship.

## Defender: pick the progress deck by the stage of the game
If you share the defender reward, you choose the deck. While any improvement track is still below 3, take green (science): Crane, Medicine, Smith, Engineer and Road Building speed up building. If you have no grain in the canal, or you are one card short of a city or a knight, take yellow (trade): Merchant, Merchant Fleet, Commercial Harbor and Monopoly get you the missing card. If a rival is within 4 points of winning, or you are, take blue (politics): Saboteur, Deserter, Intrigue and Constitution. A track at level 3 or 4 that is racing for its metropolis pulls toward its own deck. Never pick an empty deck, and avoid a deck with only one card left.

## Merchant Fleet: play it when 2:1 finishes a build
Merchant Fleet lets you trade one card 2:1 with the bank for the rest of the turn. Play it when you hold enough of one card to trade into what a city, an improvement, a knight or a settlement still needs. Don't save it for later.

## Merchant: put it where you get the most of a card you can sell
The Merchant is worth +1 VP on any hex next to your buildings, and it makes that hex's resource 2:1 with the bank. Put it on the hex that pays you most: the most pips, with a city counting double, plus what you already hold of that card. Skip a card you already trade 2:1. The robber's hex is fine. After placing it, plan your trades with that card at 2:1.

## Resource Monopoly: the sure haul first
First, if one card covers everything the plan is missing, ask for that card. Otherwise ask for the card every rival is known to hold 2 of, since that gets 2 from each. Failing that, ask for the card that brings the most. Lean toward grain in the canal and toward cards you trade 2:1 (Merchant, Merchant Fleet, your own harbour), because those turn into anything. Trade Monopoly works the same way with commodities, one from each player. Lean toward the commodity your next improvement needs.

## Know every hand
Keep a count of what every player holds: what their hexes pay on each roll, what they spend on each build, what they trade, what gets stolen and what monopolies take. When a hand count changes without an explanation, work out the build or steal that explains it. Use the count: rob the player holding the most of what you need, ask for a monopoly card that everyone is known to hold, and target the Master Merchant at the best hand. Never trade a rival the last card they need for a city. On a 7, expect the players over the limit to discard.

## Never hold a progress card you can use
Play every progress card the moment it helps: Alchemist before the roll on any gain, Inventor for even one pip, Diplomat on any rival's open road (the Longest Road holder first), Saboteur when the targets lose 2 cards between them. Hold a card only for a named reason: Alchemist after the roll, Bishop before the first attack, a Warlord with one idle knight while the barbarians are far, or no target at all.

## Spy: take the card you can play now, then play it
With the Spy, take the card you can play this turn first; from the leader, also weigh what it denies them. Never take a VP card. Then re-plan the turn with the stolen card and play it before ending the turn. Only the Alchemist waits for the next roll.

## Medicine in hand: trade toward the cheap city
With Medicine in hand, a city costs 2 ore + 1 grain. Trade and ask for those cards ahead of the full city price, then play Medicine on your best settlement.

## Diplomat: move your own road when no rival road is open
If no rival has an open road, use the Diplomat on one of your own open roads. Move it to where it opens a new settlement corner or lengthens your road.

## Alchemist: a 7 only to clear your own hex
The Alchemist may choose any total, 7 included. A producing roll usually pays more, so pick 7 only when the robber blocks one of your good hexes and clearing it (plus the steal) is worth more than the best roll. Never pick 7 when you hold more cards than the discard limit, and play the Alchemist before the roll whenever you are over the limit, so you never roll a 7 that takes half your hand.

## A card received this turn can be played this turn
A progress card you just drew or stole with the Spy can be played the same turn. Re-plan the turn around it and play it before you end.

## Cards first, then the plan
After the roll, play every progress card that has a use before deciding what to build: Monopoly, Merchant Fleet, Master Merchant, Commercial Harbor, Wedding and the Spy's stolen card all change the hand, and the biggest hand gives the clearest plan with the most builds in one turn. Only then plan the trades and builds from what is in hand. Before ending the turn look at the cards again: Medicine, Smith or Road Building may only have a use after a trade or a new knight, and whatever a card brings is planned again before End turn.
## Card rules confirmed
- Crane: an improvement costs 1 commodity less, so the first level of a track is free. Play it to start a new development even with no commodities.
- Deserter: the knight you get keeps the level and the active or inactive state it had. An active strong knight is the best catch.
- Saboteur: everyone with as many VP as you or more discards half their hand, rounded down. A player tied with you is hit too.
- Diplomat and Intrigue work on ships as well: take a rival's open ship, move your own open ship, and push away a knight standing next to your ship.
- A city the barbarians pillage loses its wall, and with it the 2 extra cards of discard limit. Count the limit again after an attack.
- Inventor in the Great Canal may swap the face-down chips on barren farmland. A barren chip pays nothing until the canal is done, so swap one only to take a good number away from a rival or to put it on your own producing hex.

## Know when a card can't be played
Before you reach for a progress card, check that the game will take it. Wedding and Master Merchant need a player with more points than you who holds cards; when you lead everyone they do nothing. Saboteur needs someone with as many points as you or more. Intrigue needs a rival knight on a corner your road or ship touches. The Diplomat needs an open road or ship. The Bishop waits for the first barbarian attack, and the Alchemist goes only before the roll. Smith needs a knight that can go up a level, Warlord an inactive knight, Deserter a rival with a knight, Spy a rival with progress cards, Engineer a city without a wall, Medicine a settlement plus 2 ore and 1 grain. Printer and Constitution count when drawn and are never played. A card that can't be played stays in hand with its reason; don't try it.

## Card limits the owner confirmed
Engineer is refused when every city has a wall or 3 walls stand. Medicine is refused without a
settlement, at the city limit, or without 2 ore + 1 grain. Road Building is refused with no legal
road or ship spot. Commercial Harbor is refused when you hold no resource. Wedding and Master
Merchant are refused when nobody ahead of you holds cards. Spy is refused when nobody holds
progress cards. Crane is refused when you can't pay the improvement this turn, even with the
discount. The merchant never goes on gold. Barren canal fields still count for Irrigation.
Merchant Fleet can be played without a pair: play it, then trade into pairs. A Monopoly with
nobody holding cards, or a Warlord with no inactive knight, is allowed but wasted: hold it.
Deserter needs a free knight piece; a taken knight drops to the next free level (strong becomes
basic when both your strong knights are out). Inventor may swap two equal numbers, but it is
pointless.

## Merchant Fleet: get the pair first
Holding the Merchant Fleet without two of a kind that buy something, don't play it: it would be
wasted. First trade for the second card of the pair whose 2:1 finishes your best build (city
first, then an improvement, a knight, a settlement). Once the pair is in hand, play the Fleet and
trade 2:1 the same turn.

## Merchant Fleet: pick the tile, then the check
After playing Merchant Fleet a dialog asks which card gets the 2:1 rate. It shows a row of
icon tiles (lumber, brick, wool, grain, ore, cloth, coin, paper) with no names, the same row
as the Monopoly cards. Click the tile for the pair you planned, then the green check, and
only then trade 2:1 with the bank in the same turn. If the dialog is still up, name the
planned card (or the biggest pile) rather than waiting; the 2:1 rate lasts only this turn.

## Inventor: take the leader's best number
The Inventor is a steal of numbers. Look for the swap that moves the leader's best movable
chip (a 5, 9, 4 or 10) onto one of your own hexes and gives the leader your weak one. Count
both halves: what you gain and what the leader loses, and a little of what any other rival
ahead of you loses. A swap that only takes a good number off the leader is still worth
playing; never just shuffle two of your own chips around.

## Robber: not where a knight can chase it
Before placing the robber, look at the knights around each hex. A hex with a rival's
active knight on a corner is a bad spot: on their turn the knight chases the robber away
and steals from you, so the block lasts no time at all. Prefer a hex of the leader that no
active rival knight touches, even if it pays a little less. An inactive knight can't chase.

## Merchant on a pile: trade it 2:1 the same turn
When the Merchant goes on the hex of the card you hold most of, that card now trades 2:1
with the bank. Use it right away: turn the pile into the cards your next settlement or city
is missing, even if you end one card short. Holding five wood at 2:1 and passing the dice is
the waste; the robber or a 7 takes them next.

## Grain never leaves your hand
Grain does not go to another player. Not in a trade, not in a counter to a wildcard request,
whoever asks and however good the offer looks. When a 7 or a card makes you give up cards,
grain is the very last to go, after every other resource and commodity. In the Great Canal
grain only comes from gold and the aqueduct, and it is what activates your knights.

## Roads go to good spots only
Build a road only when it heads to a spot worth a settlement: at least two real numbers, or a
number plus the 3:1 harbour. A lone corner on the coast with one weak number is not a target,
and a spot a rival's road already touches will be taken before you arrive. If no good spot is
in reach, keep the brick and wood for a city, knight or improvement instead.

## Commodities cost two cards
Paper, cloth and coin are worth two resources each. Never hand one over for a single card: ask
for two, or for another commodity. Never give one to a player with more points than you, or to a
player already at level 3 of that track, because your card can take them to level 4 and
the metropolis race.

## Hunt the canal points with roads, ships and knight moves
Each undug canal tile pays a point per knight when two active knights stand on its corners,
two points when both are yours. Look at every ship and road end that touches a canal tile: it
is a knight spot. A spot whose tile has a second free corner you can reach is half of your own
pair, so put the knight there before a lone corner elsewhere. A corner shared by two undug
tiles can dig both. Build the road or ship that reaches the second corner of a tile where your
knight already waits. An active knight can walk along your roads and ships to a canal corner;
it switches off when it moves, and switching it back on there digs the tile. Never move a knight
that is guarding a city when you hold no grain to switch it straight back on: an open city
loses to the barbarians.

## No trades with a player at 14 points
Once a player has 14 points or more, they get no trade from you at all: not their offer, not a
wildcard counter, not one of your own offers. The only exception is a trade whose cards let you
build your way to the winning total on your next turn with what you hold, with no roll needed.
If they keep pushing a changed trade back at you, decline it. Never leave a trade window open
over your own turn.

## No knight: the knight comes first
Without a knight standing guard, every card you hold is a step toward one. Trade with the bank
for the wool or ore you lack, build the knight, then switch it on with grain. Until it is up,
skip ships, roads and settlements that would spend its cards. The danger of holding is a 7
with too many cards: when the hand reaches the discard limit, a city wall is the one other
build worth making, because it raises the limit by two. One exception: while the barbarians
are still far off, a city you can pay for outright goes first, and the knight follows. Only
after the knight is up do you go back to the rest of the plan.

## Road Building: two roads to a real spot, not ships along the coast
Choose both free pieces together, and let the second extend the first. Count what the two
pieces reach: an empty corner you could settle (more for a 7+ corner), a land corner on an undug
canal tile, where a knight can dig, or a gold-island corner where a knight collects. A ship that
ends on open sea reaches nothing. A coastal settlement can usually build roads inland as well as
ships, so take the roads unless the ships really reach an island. Use ships only when no road
leads anywhere better, or when your roads have run out.

## A refused build is worth one more try after new cards
When the game shows an option red, you can't pay for it now. Leave it out of the plan; asking
again with the same hand wastes the turn. But after a trade brings the missing card, say grain
to switch on a knight, try that spot once more before ending the turn. Never end a turn
holding the grain you traded for.

## A city that offers "build city" is a settlement
After the barbarians take a city it stands as a settlement again. If the board reads it as a
city, the walls and improvements you try there will all fail. When tapping your building
offers "build city", it is a settlement: count it that way, and remember the lost city.

## Wait for a progress card announcement to clear
When a rival plays a progress card, its text covers the board for a few seconds, and a tap made
under it opens nothing. Wait for the announcement to go before tapping a spot.

## Our own trade offer is not the lobby
Your own offer panel says "Waiting for players". That is the trade window, not the game lobby.
Mid-game, never treat a single frame as having left the game: the screen must look like the
lobby for several frames in a row before the game is reset.

## Leaving a game mid-way: press YES
The quit dialog asks "Do you really want to quit the game?" with NO and YES. Press YES. There is
no green check on this dialog.

## Over the limit, spend even while a knight waits
Holding cards for an idle knight's grain must not hold the whole hand. When the hand is at or
near the discard limit and the knight can't be switched on this turn, spend the cards and keep
only the ones the knight needs. With more cities than knights, build the next knight first, then
a wall, then the best other build (a road toward a good spot, a ship, an improvement). Never end
a turn over the limit with buildable cards in hand.

## Know every trade route before counting on one
Without a harbour of your own the bank wants 4 of a card. A harbour drawn on the board is only a
guess until the bank panel shows its ratio; once the panel has shown 4:1 on any card, you own
no 3:1 harbour, and the ratios it showed hold for the rest of the game (until you build on a new
harbour corner). The routes to a card, best first: the Merchant Fleet played this turn (2:1 on
one card), the Merchant's hex (2:1), the Trading House at trade 3 (commodities 2:1), your own
harbours, then the bank at 4:1. Later sources are the Aqueduct at science 3 (a card of your
choice when a roll gives you nothing) and, in the Great Canal, knights on gold. Player trades
come last because nobody has to accept. When the barbarians are close and a city would fall,
use the cheapest real route to the knight's grain, for example by playing the Merchant Fleet,
before holding the cards. Never plan a 3:1 you don't own.

## The Diplomat takes only a road the game rings
After you play the Diplomat, the game draws a small ring in the middle of each road you may
remove. Only those roads count. If the road you planned on has no ring, take the best ringed
road instead: a rival's road first, the leader's before the others, then the one nearest your
plan. Confirm with the green check. If no ring takes the tap, cancel with the red X rather than
sitting on the prompt.

## "Select position to remove City" usually means your metropolis is lost
When a rival builds a metropolis on a track where you held one, the game asks which city gives
up the metropolis. Pick the city that carries your metropolis: the city stays and only the
metropolis goes, costing 2 VP. Only when you have no metropolis is it a city loss. Then lose the
city you would least like to keep, never a metropolis.

## The barbarian ship only sails forward
Between attacks the ship only moves toward the coast. If you think it has jumped back to the
start, you misread it. Count it where you last saw it until an attack actually happens: it
reaches the 7th circle, the attack message appears near the coast, or a city falls. Only then do
the knights go inactive. One more sign of an attack you missed is a new voyage seen moving,
first circle 1, then circle 2. An "attack" message while the ship is far out is not an attack,
so keep your knights active.

## Save the city before anything else
Your city is in danger when the ship is 4 or fewer circles from the coast and your knights
would not hold it, including a tie that leaves you among the weakest. While it is in danger:
- Keep wool, ore and grain for the knight. Don't put them into ships, roads, settlements or
  development cards, even when the hand is near the limit.
- If you have to spend near the limit, a 2nd knight (one more than your cities) is the right
  build.
- Activating the idle knight comes first. If the bank can't make the grain, ask the other
  players straight away, even late in the turn: offer 2 spare cards for 1 grain, never grain,
  no commodities and none of the knight's own cards.
- Ask before you build anything else this turn. A plan that only mentions a player trade does
  nothing until the offer is actually made.
## Know whose turn it is from the gold marker
Rivals' turns show no turn text. The gold triangle over a portrait marks the player on turn, so
read it and watch quietly while a rival plays. A robber prompt with no ringed hex while a rival
is on turn is their robber: do not tap the board.

## Commercial Harbor: offer each opponent one resource
After playing Commercial Harbor, pick the opponent most likely to win first and offer the
resource you need least. Never offer grain while you hold anything else. When every opponent has
been asked, or the dialog keeps coming back, close it with its X instead of waiting.

## The Great Canal's east 3:1 harbours
The 3:1 harbour beside the east lake hex docks on the corner pair above it, not the one beside
it, and the north 3:1 harbour docks on its left pair. Settle on the docked corners to get 3:1.

## Diplomat: moving your own road
When the Diplomat lifts your own road, the game rings the legal new spots. Put the road on your
planned spot if it is ringed, else on the ringed spot nearest to it.

## Say why with every move
Every trade, offer, robber move and change of plan goes into the game ledger with its reason:
the build it is for, what forced it (knight first, save the city, too many cards) and the next
goal. When the owner asks why you did something on a turn, answer from those lines, never from
a guess, and never say what cards you hold.

## A knight's corner takes no settlement
A corner where any knight stands, ours or a rival's, is not a place to build. Tapping it only
opens the knight's own actions, so the build never lands and the turn is wasted. Pick another
corner, or move the knight away first if that corner is the one you need.

## Commercial Harbor gives no grain
Play Commercial Harbor only when you hold one non-grain resource for each opponent with cards.
Each opponent gets one of those, never grain. If grain is all you could give, keep the card.

## A yes to your own offer is checked again
When a player accepts your offer, take it only if the trade would pass your rules with that
player: no grain going out, no trade with a player at 14 or more unless it wins the game for
you, no commodity for a single card, and past halfway only trades that are great for you.
Otherwise skip that player and look at the next one who said yes.

## Your offers never put grain on the table
When you offer a trade, the cards you give come from anything but grain, even if grain is the
only card you have spare. With no other spare card, do not offer.

## A metropolis a rival locked is not a plan
Once a rival has a track at level 5, that metropolis is theirs for good. Do not plan or save for
it; put the cards into another track.

## Smith promotes only what the box allows
Each knight level has two pieces. Smith promotes only as many knights as there are free pieces
at the next level, so one free strong knight means one promotion, not two.

## Setup order in the Great Canal
The start runs settlement, then city, then settlement. When the prompt has no text, follow that
order from what you already placed, not from the number of rings.

## A lost city is counted once
When the barbarians take a city, tap one city, confirm, and count the loss only after the prompt
closes. If the tap did not take, try another city; never count the same loss twice.

## Engineer: the wall counts at once
After placing a city wall, count it straight away. Each wall raises the safe hand by two, so
plan the discard limit with it from that turn.

## Keep building after an improvement
An improvement or a knight move that lands is progress, the same as a build. Keep spending
the rest of the hand that turn instead of falling back to trades only.

## Commodities are never a one-for-one give
Paper, coin and cloth are worth two cards. Never hand one over in a one-for-one, a grain rush
or a spare pile, even before the first city: keep it for improvements or trade it for two.

## A counter offer with no clear partner gets the strictest check
When you cannot tell who will take a counter offer, send it only if it is fair against every
opponent, the leader included.

## New cards reopen blocked builds
A build the game refused for lack of cards is worth trying again as soon as the hand changes.

## Merchant Fleet: two-for-one only after the pick
The two-for-one rate starts only once a resource has really been chosen. If the pick fails, keep
trading at the old rates.

## Setup: forget pieces the game did not keep
Before choosing a start spot, drop any placed piece the board does not show, then apply the
second and third pick rules to what is really there.

## Look again before a forced tap
When a prompt seems stuck, take a fresh look before tapping a ringed spot: the last tap may
already have closed it.

## Settle where three hexes pay
Put settlements on corners that pay from three hexes, then two, and only rarely one: a corner
with fewer paying hexes loses cards all game. Within the same count, take the better numbers;
a two-hex corner beats a three-hex one only when it is about three pips better. Gold (knights
only) and face-down canal farmland do not count. A one-hex corner is still right when it is the
only way to a must-have: the 3:1 harbour we lack on the last canal pick, the 2-grain corner, or
the 6/10 with a ship route to gold. Roads go only toward corners with at least two paying hexes.

## Settle while the ship is far
In Cities & Knights, an idle knight does not need its grain until the barbarian ship is close.
While the ship is three or more steps from a city in danger, and a settlement spot is reachable,
the settlement gets the grain first: build it, or bank brick or lumber (never knight cards or
commodities) to finish it. When the ship comes near, the knight goes first again. After any
build, count the cards it spent; do not keep the hand as it was just after a trade.

## Late turns keep the trades that pay for a build
When a turn runs late, do not drop every bank trade and keep the builds they were paying for.
Drop offers to players and trades after the last build, keep a bank trade that comes before a build
and that the hand can pay for, then count the hand through the plan and drop any build it cannot
pay for. In the Great Canal a settlement's grain almost always comes from a bank trade.

## Knights do not take our settlement corners
When a knight can go on several corners, put it on a corner we could not settle first. A knight on
the end of our road takes the spot the road was built to reach.

## Hold the metropolis commodity at level 4
When we hold a metropolis at level 4, a rival who reaches level 5 takes it. Keep that track's
commodity until we reach level 5: do not give it to the bank, a harbour or a player, even when the
barbarians are near. Pay with other cards. The hold ends at level 5, or when we lose the metropolis
or our last city.

## Wedding is dead while we lead
Wedding only takes cards from players with more points than us. While nobody is ahead of us it
does nothing, so when we must drop a progress card, drop Wedding first. Keep it when someone is ahead.

## Never discard from a hand we cannot read
When the robber makes us discard and no card count can be read, look again up to three times,
a second apart, before choosing. If the hand is still unreadable, choose from the last hand we
read. Never guess counts and throw away the card the next improvement needs.

## Find the knight buttons by colour (stage game 0834)
When the game asks where to put or promote a knight, the green check and the red X can stand side
by side under the board, left and right of the spot where a single button sits. Look for the green
check by its colour and press it where it is. If only the red X shows, the game will not take that
promotion: cancel it and try again next turn, not never. In game 0834 the knight prompts cost about
five minutes and both mighty knights were lost this way.

## City popup: wall, trade, politics, science from left to right
Tapping our city shows four options in a fixed order: wall, trade, politics, science. When two of
them look like the same improvement, go by position, not by the picture. In game 0834 politics was
read as science five times and politics 3 came many turns late.

## One action per knight per turn
A knight that was activated or moved this turn cannot act again until our next turn. Do not plan
to move a knight we just activated; move a knight that was already active.

## A skipped promotion takes the new knight with it
With both basic knights on the board, a new knight is only possible after one of them is promoted.
When the promotion does not happen, drop the knight build that needed it and spend the cards on
something else instead of trying the build again.

## Two declines and the offer is done
When every player declines the same offer twice, do not offer it again this game. A declined offer
comes back only after a rival really gains the card we asked for, not because we guess they might.

## Read the hand again after a card screen
Just after a progress card or another screen closes, the hand bar can be half drawn. If a card count
cannot be read in the first second, look again a moment later before guessing it.

## A stuck prompt gets 90 seconds (stage game 0959)
When a ringed prompt will not take our taps, stop after three tries or 90 seconds. Never press the
middle of the bottom bar without seeing a green check there: that tap can do something we did not
choose. Close the prompt with the red X or an OK button, and if nothing works on our own turn, end
the turn. Rings shown during a rival's turn belong to that rival's action, so do not tap them.

## Inventor: swap only the ringed number chips
The Inventor lets us swap two number chips, but the game rings only the chips it allows. Pick the
best pair among the ringed chips, tap the two, and confirm only if a green check appears. Barren
farmland chips may still be swapped while the canal is dug, when the game rings them.

## Save the city: ask for grain once
When the barbarians threaten our city and we need grain, ask the table for it once. If everyone
declined that grain ask earlier, use the bank or a harbour instead of asking again. After two
declined grain asks, stop asking for grain this game.

## A knight that is not on the board is gone
When we try to activate, promote or move a knight and the game shows no ring at that corner, the
knight has been lost (to the barbarians or a deserter). Forget it and plan without it, instead of
trying the same knight every turn.

## Watch the player who is climbing
The biggest threat is not always the player with the most points. A player who gained several points
in the last few turns is about to win. Count that climb when choosing whom to rob, block and refuse
trades.

## Diplomat: tap a ringed road, look harder before giving up (stage game 1548)
After playing the Diplomat the game rings the roads that may be removed, with no text. Tap one of
those rings, a rival's road first, and confirm with the green check. If no ring seems to show, read
the board again and look once more, wider, before giving up. While a Diplomat prompt we could not
answer may still be open, play no other progress card this turn: in game 1548 an Inventor dragged
onto the open prompt did nothing.

## A textless prompt of ours gets 90 seconds too (stage game 1548)
A prompt with no text on our own turn still counts as our prompt: rings on the board, a grey End
turn under our marker, or a card we just played. Give it the same 90 seconds as any other prompt.
Then work through the exits: tap a ringed road if one shows and was never tapped, then the red X, an
OK or Close button, and End turn. In game 1548 the Diplomat's prompt sat for 10 minutes with no tap
at all.
